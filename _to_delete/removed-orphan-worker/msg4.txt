fix(reports): scheduled reports were waiting for a cron nobody had written down

Raised as a question at the end of the third sweep and answered here: the
`scheduled-report` worker being enqueued by nothing was not by design.

It has been registered since reports shipped. Its header deferred the trigger to
"an app scheduled-task or external cron" — a dependency recorded in a comment in
one handler and nowhere else, on a fleet where every other periodic job is
registered in scheduleRecurring(). So a tenant who scheduled a weekly
receivables report got a scheduled_report row, watched next_run_at arrive and
then sit in the past, and received nothing, unless somebody remembered to POST
/reports/scheduled/run-due by hand.

This is the same defect regie-aging-scheduler exists to fix, in the same
directory, and its own header says so: "`regie-aging` has been a registered
worker since the régie module shipped and NOTHING EVER ENQUEUED IT." That fix
was made once and the pattern was never swept for. It has been now.

scheduled-report-scheduler fans out one job per live tenant.

HOURLY, not daily, because the tick interval IS the resolution of the feature.
`next_run_at` is a timestamp and the due query asks `next_run_at <= now()`, so a
daily tick makes every cadence a tenant can choose — daily, weekly, monthly,
quarterly — mean "whenever the fleet cron happens to fire". At five past the
hour, clear of the pile every other scheduler puts on :00. One cheap indexed
range scan per tenant per hour, returning nothing 23 times out of 24.

LIVE only. Reports email people. A Test run would generate the report, have its
mail suppressed by email.service's sandbox guard, and still consume next_run_at
— a rehearsal that spends the schedule without rehearsing anything. Test stays
reachable through the route, the same line contract-lapse and regie-aging draw.

Per-tenant-per-hour jobId so a tenant whose reports outlast the interval never
piles up, and `live-<hour>` rather than `live:<hour>` because BullMQ allows a
colon in a custom id only when it splits into exactly three segments. One
attempt: re-running the sweep on a transient failure would re-generate reports
whose emails already went out, and those emails carry their own retries. The
next hour's tick is the retry.

The orphan sweep's externally-driven allowance is now EMPTY, and it gained a
test asserting that every `-scheduler` worker is actually ticked by
scheduleRecurring — a cron handler nobody enqueues being precisely how this one
spent its life.
